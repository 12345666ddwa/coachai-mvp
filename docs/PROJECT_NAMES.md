# Project Name Candidates
**For team vote - prepared by Xing (Happy) | 16 Sep 2026**

Why a rename: the current working name "CoachAI" was a placeholder. We have compiled
alternatives that are specific, memorable, and avoid generic "AI-startup" naming patterns
(no "-AI" suffix, no buzzwords, no template words like "Hub / Genius / Mentor / Mind").

Each candidate is a real word or established term with a concrete image - the way physical
products are named. Please vote for your top 2, and note any that you actively dislike.

---

## Shortlist (10)

### 1. Redpen
- **Image**: the red pen every teacher marks with.
- **Why it works**: instantly understood by anyone in education; concrete object; the
  product's whole job is the red-pen workflow. Two syllables, easy to say.
- **Possible concern**: many school suppliers use red-pen imagery in marketing (as a
  concept, not usually as a name).

### 2. Margin
- **Image**: the margin of a page - where teachers write comments.
- **Why it works**: double meaning: the space for feedback, and the "margin" a student
  needs to close. Calm, editorial, premium feel.
- **Possible concern**: common English word; may need a qualifier for the domain
  (e.g. margin.education).

### 3. Dotpoint
- **Image**: "dot points" - the syllabus structure every NSW teacher works from.
- **Why it works**: hyper-local to NSW education; a teacher sees the name and immediately
  knows the product speaks their language. Directly matches the lesson-planner feature
  (built from syllabus dot points).
- **Possible concern**: Australian-specific; less meaningful outside NSW (fine for now).

### 4. Markbench
- **Image**: marking + benchmark (a standard to measure against).
- **Why it works**: sounds like a serious instrument, not a chatbot. Combines the two core
  concepts: marking and standards. Compact, tech-forward without buzzwords.
- **Possible concern**: invented compound - needs a quick explanation the first time.

### 5. Arbiter
- **Image**: a fair judge who settles a dispute.
- **Why it works**: reflects our architecture precisely - the Verifier agent is literally a
  second, independent arbiter of every mark. Sounds established and trustworthy.
- **Possible concern**: slightly formal; some may find it cold.

### 6. Secondmark
- **Image**: "second marker" - the real exam-system practice of a second examiner
  checking the first marker's work.
- **Why it works**: this is exactly what our dual-agent design does. An education
  insider term with a built-in story. Has "mark" in it without sounding like a product gimmick.
- **Possible concern**: reads as two words; check spelling confusion.

### 7. Underline
- **Image**: underlining the line that matters.
- **Why it works**: a teacher's gesture of emphasis; clean single word; implies "we point
  out what matters" (strengths, weaknesses, next steps).
- **Possible concern**: generic verb; several small apps share the name.

### 8. Fairmark
- **Image**: fair marking.
- **Why it works**: names the promise directly - consistent, unbiased marking for every
  student. Good for the equity angle (closing educational disparities).
- **Possible concern**: slightly plain; sounds like a bank (Fairmark was an investment
  platform) - check.

### 9. Cairn
- **Image**: a stack of stones that marks a trail.
- **Why it works**: short, distinctive, memorable; a marker that guides a journey - exactly
  the student-tracker idea (progress markers along the learning path).
- **Possible concern**: meaning needs one sentence of explanation.

### 10. Nib
- **Image**: the nib of a pen.
- **Why it works**: three letters, crisp, unique-as-a-name; the writing instrument is
  implicit without being literal (no red-pen cliché).
- **Possible concern**: very short; might read as an acronym; check international meaning.

---

## Elimination notes (so the team knows what we avoided)
- Rejected: [something]AI names, "Coach/Sense/Genius/Mind/Path/Hub" compounds, and words
  already heavily used by edtech products (Beacon, Compass, Quill, Chalk - all taken by
  existing companies).
- Criterion: (1) says what it does or evokes the right object, (2) survives being said
  aloud once, (3) does not smell like an AI template product, (4) available-ish.
